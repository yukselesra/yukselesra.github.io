I study atomic nuclei under conditions that cannot be reproduced in a laboratory — extreme proton–neutron asymmetry near the drip lines, and the temperatures of billions of kelvin reached in core-collapse supernovae and neutron star mergers — and connect those calculations to what can actually be measured.

Research

My framework is nuclear energy density functional (EDF) theory, in both relativistic and non-relativistic formulations, together with the random phase approximation methods built on top of it.

Constraining the functionals

Optimising nuclear energy density functionals against ground-state properties, collective excitation data, and the parity-violating electron scattering measurements CREX and PREX-II. This line of work produced the DD-PCX parametrisation.

Nuclei at finite temperature

Finite-temperature extensions of the (quasiparticle) random phase approximation, including the charge-exchange channel. These make it possible to compute electric and magnetic dipole strength, γ-ray strength functions and Gamow–Teller transitions in hot nuclei. Applied globally, they showed that the limits of nuclear existence are not fixed, but shift with temperature.

Weak interaction rates in stellar environments

Electron capture cross sections and β-decay half-lives under the conditions of core-collapse supernovae and neutron star mergers, where nuclear structure input controls the deleptonisation of the collapsing core.

From nuclei to neutron stars

Linking finite-nuclei observables — dipole polarizability, neutron skin thickness, the symmetry energy — to neutron star radii, tidal deformabilities and compactness, connecting terrestrial experiments to multimessenger astronomy.

Machine learning

Neural networks, support vector regression and Gaussian process regression applied to nuclear masses and related observables, with attention to what these models can and cannot extrapolate away from the stability line.

Joining

I am glad to hear from prospective PhD students interested in nuclear structure theory, nuclear astrophysics, or machine learning applied to nuclear data.
